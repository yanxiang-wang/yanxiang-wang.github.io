# Academic Paper Rebuttal Guide

## General Principles

**Be concise.** Program committee members have many rebuttals to read, so respect their time.

**Make information easy to find.** Use clear section titles and organize content logically.

**Stay professional.** Be reasonable and collaborative - show that you'll be pleasant to work with during shepherding. Confrontational rebuttals can cause borderline papers to be rejected.

**Always be positive.** For everything you write, ask yourself if you can remove negative spin. The glass is always half full (while still acknowledging limitations when appropriate).

## Organization and Structure

### Recommended Structure

1. Brief acknowledgment of reviewers (keep it simple: "We thank the reviewers for their thorough comments")
2. **First half:** Pick the 3 most important questions raised across all reviews and focus on answering them (don't rebut sequentially line by line)
3. **Second half:** Address secondary topics or topics raised by single reviewers
4. Organize remaining points by topic, with reviewer numbers in section titles
5. End with "Misc." section for minor points

### Flat Hierarchy Rule

Use a flat organization with one twist: you can have N top-level points where only the last point ("Misc.") has subpoints.

## Writing Style Guidelines

### Do This

- **Provide information directly** when reviewers ask for it
- **Use concrete numbers** and experimental results to address issues
- **Be self-contained** - make every sentence understandable without cross-referencing
- **Start with winnable points** that are both important and defensible
- **Say "we agree"** instead of "we acknowledge" (which implies fault)

### Avoid This

- **Don't quote criticisms** from reviews - no need for PC members to read negative points twice
- **Don't be confrontational** or comment on reviewer intelligence
- **Don't apologize excessively**
- **Don't whine or complain** about workload constraints
- **Don't speculate** about future work - stick to your current submission
- **Don't mention "final version"** (implies acceptance) - say "revised version" instead
- **Don't include long introductions** or conclusions
- **Don't quote positive reviews** to play reviewers against each other

## Content Guidelines

### Addressing Reviewer Comments

- **Address all points** - you don't know which are important to which reviewers
- **Clarify missed points** without implying reviewer error - if they missed it, it probably wasn't clear
- **Provide requested information** directly - don't refer to other papers
- **Focus on technical points** over opinions
- **Answer directly** - don't say "Reviewer X asks if..." just give the answer

### Technical Responses

- **Don't hesitate to code and experiment** - concrete numbers are powerful
- **Avoid work-in-progress claims** - don't say "we are working on extension X" as they may require completion
- **Make assertions, not speculations** - avoid "we could do this, but then this bad thing might happen"

## Before Writing

### Review Analysis

- **Sort reviews by question and reviewer** to understand overall feedback patterns
- **Sort by both question# and reviewer#** to get a better sense of overall feedback
- **Remember reviews are scrutinized** by primary/secondary reviewers who either agree or await your response
- **Reviews are in random order** - it's nearly impossible to figure out which is from primary/secondary
- **Don't be confrontational** toward any individual reviewer - primaries/secondaries either agree with reviews or are waiting for your response

### Time Investment

Possibly dedicate the full rebuttal period (typically 4 days) - this is extremely important for your paper's success.

## Strategic Considerations

### High-Score Papers

Focus on demonstrating you're willing to work collaboratively on revisions and address technical concerns.

### Low-Score Papers

Consider whether to continue or withdraw to be fair to the review process. If continuing, focus on:

- Correcting misconceptions about contribution magnitude and potential overlap
- Preparing ground for resubmission by addressing core issues
- **Avoid** providing more details about immature technical aspects

### After Rebuttal

Primary/secondary and 3 tertiary reviewers will discuss your response online during the next week. Your goal is to give supportive reviewers ammunition to convince others.

**You're expected to address factual errors** but can also demonstrate willingness to work with primary/secondary during revision. It's okay to admit weaknesses and suggest improvements to exposition.

**For papers with low scores:** Consider withdrawing to be fair to the review process and let reviewers focus on other papers. You can withdraw by contacting the papers administrator.

## Final Checklist

- [ ] All reviewer comments addressed
- [ ] Information easy to find with clear sections
- [ ] No confrontational language
- [ ] Positive tone throughout
- [ ] Concrete evidence provided where possible
- [ ] Text proofread
- [ ] Final pass completed to verify all comments addressed
- [ ] Reviews saved for potential future revisions (unclear how long conference systems keep them available)

## Remember

Your rebuttal should demonstrate that you're reasonable authors who will be collaborative during the revision process. Focus on technical substance while maintaining a professional, positive tone throughout.

## Ramesh's Additional Points

### Writing the Rebuttal

- Sort reviews by question# and by reviewer# to get a better sense of the overall feedback
- In the first half of your rebuttal, try to pick the 3 most important questions raised in the reviews and focus on answering them. Do not rebut reviews sequentially line by line.
- In the second half, you can try to address secondary topics or topics raised by a single reviewer
- Make it easy to read by ensuring every sentence is self-contained (if you refer to a reviewer comment, try to give some context because the PC member won't have patience to cross-check.)

### Before Rebuttal

- The reviews are usually scrutinized by primary/secondary before they are made available to you. So remember that, primary/secondary either agree with what is in the review or are waiting to see how you respond to the raised issues. Do not be confrontational towards any individual reviewer.
- The reviews are in random order. It is nearly impossible to figure out from the reviews which one is from primary/secondary.

### After Rebuttal

- Primary/secondary and 3 tertiary reviewers will discuss online your rebuttal during the next week
- Your goal at this stage is to give supportive reviewers enough ammunition so that they can convince the others.
- You are expected to address only factual errors but you can also use the opportunity to demonstrate that, if the paper is accepted, you are willing to work with the primary/secondary during the revision. So it is ok to admit the weaknesses of the paper and suggest how you will improve the exposition.
- Finally, if the scores are low, it is fine to withdraw. A lot of work goes into the review process. You want to be fair to the process and let the reviewers focus on other papers.
